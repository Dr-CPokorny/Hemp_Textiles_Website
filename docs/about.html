Make it With Hemp

Building a more sustainable future for the textile arts.

Project Overview

"Make It With Hemp" began as a student project in Oregon State University's Design for Social Impact (DSI 415) course. Students and faculty worked together to explore how hemp fabrics might fit into quiltmaking practices. Since then, the project has grown into a research and outreach effort focused on hemp fabrics, quilting, fiber arts, and sustainable making. Through research, community engagement, and educational resources, we aim to learn how makers, retailers, and consumers view hemp fabrics and how they might fit into the future of textile arts.

The U.S. quilting industry is a major part of the craft economy, generating $4.5 billion in annual revenue and supporting 9–11 million active quilters in 2025 [R-023]. Cotton remains the most common fiber used in quiltmaking, but many quilters are also exploring other natural fibers, textured woven fabrics, and linen blends that add texture, depth, and visual interest to their work [R-023, R-024, R-025]. At the same time, growing interest in sustainability and thoughtful material choices has encouraged conversations about a wider range of fabric options within the quiltmaking community.

Hemp is one of the natural fibers gaining attention in the broader textile industry. The U.S. hemp fiber market is expected to grow from $120.4 million in 2024 to $360.2 million by 2033, showing increased interest in new uses for hemp-based materials [R-026]. Hemp fabricss are known for being durable, breathable, and biodegradable, and hemp can be grown using relatively low amounts of water and pesticides [R-006, R-009, R-012, R-014]. Even with this growing interest, hemp fabrics are still uncommon in quiltmaking. Limited availability, unfamiliarity with the material, historical associations with marijuana, and misconceptions about texture and softness continue to influence how people view hemp and whether they choose to use it.

Quiltmaking is an ideal way to explore perceptions of materials because it is a deeply tactile, material-driven craft where makers handle, test, and compare fabrics through hands-on making. Through “Make it with Hemp,” we hope to learn how quiltmakers, quilt shop owners, and other members of the fiber arts community view hemp fabrics, what opportunities and challenges they see, and what factors influence their adoption and use. By combining research, education, and community engagement, we hope to support conversations about material choice, sustainability, and innovation in the textile arts. Most importantly, the experiences and perspectives shared by the quiltmaking community will help guide future research and improve our understanding of how hemp fabrics may work alongside other fibers in quilting and beyond. Only together, can we stitch a more sustainable future.

Meet the Team

spa

Dr. Colleen Gelhaus Pokorny

Project Lead

Dr. Pokorny is an Assistant Professor of Apparel Design at Oregon State University and a lifelong quilter. She is a member of the Textile & Apparel Innovation Research Consortia with the Global Hemp Innovation Center. Her creative scholarship uses quiltmaking to explore material‑driven sustainability and how hands‑on engagement with hemp fabrics can shift design practices and perceptions. Dr. Pokorny's work has received international recognition, including the Sandra Hutton Award for Excellence in Fiber Arts at the 2025 ITAA Design Exhibition. Her work has been shown at the National Quilt Museum, QuiltCon, AQSG Quilt Week, and the American Quilt Study Group's traveling exhibition "Quiltmakers and Designers: 1945–1979."
